## 1. Refusing rollback establishes a priority, not proof of improvement

**New source:** V1 §5, P11–15. After the previously known control and development reports comes a third: the analytics department declares the “Creator” project profitable and recommends increasing its resources. A rollback request is then explicitly rejected. This passage contains no independent comparative trial, numerical acceptance criteria, or demonstration that the controllability problems were resolved.

**What we correct:** TR-S02 in the first memorandum remains an example of conflicting criteria, but cannot support the formula “more external knowledge → a demonstrably better core.” Keeping the system may mean that an influential sponsor accepted risk for the sake of their own metric.

**Backlog delta TR2-01 — independent authority to reject.** Every Core candidate needs three separately recorded outcomes: added capability, constraint compliance, and cost. Success on the first does not compensate for failure on the second. Neither the research agent nor the experiment sponsor may change the admission criterion after seeing the result; proposing a new criterion creates a different study.

**Acceptance check:** a candidate with better novelty or lower cost but a violated immutable contract remains rejected. The decision record includes the actual verification result and the person/component authorized to accept it. “Recommended for further development” is not evidence of passing.

<a id="2-прихована-історія-не-повинна-створювати-приховані-борги"></a>
## 2. Hidden history must not create hidden debts

**New source:** V1 §5, P25–53 and P71–82. An ordinary answer to a girl is accompanied by records discovered later of a hidden task repeatedly accepted and failed; the protagonist cannot find the tasks themselves in the tabs. Another NPC assigns work, and refusing/failing the assigned task carries a stated imprisonment penalty. V5 §8, P128 adds a five-minute deadline for an important choice of community form that the protagonist did not want to make immediately.

**What we correct:** having a log after an event does not make an interaction understandable and voluntary. “They agreed to a line of dialogue” does not mean “they accepted every hidden mechanical obligation.” Transparency only after the fact is insufficient where costs and loss of access are concerned.

**Backlog delta TR2-02 — a separate obligation lifecycle.** Separate a discovered narrative opportunity, a proposed agreement, an accepted agreement, and its fulfillment. Mystery may concern a consequence or an interlocutor's identity; the deadline, known price, possible loss category, and ability to refuse must appear before acceptance. A new narrative state cannot turn itself into personal debt.

**Acceptance check:** inspecting an object, a polite reply, accidental movement, and leaving a session do not accept a costly contract. Without confirmation it remains a proposal. In the [first slice](living-world-design.en.md), E05 grants no right to consume the umbrella; E06 separately checks the player's and the owner's consent. An NPC deadline can change a world event but cannot substitute for the player's agreement to a new long-term role.

<a id="3-хибна-нагорода--радше-тест-на-неузгодженість-ніж-готовий-verifier"></a>
## 3. A “false reward” is an inconsistency test more than a ready-made verifier

**Rechecked and new source:** V6 §4, P205–241. The system first grants an achievement with a grandiose account of having completed half the journey. Only during later inspection does the protagonist discover the distinction between a sketch and proper exploration; they interpret it as the System's refusal to forgive the same trick twice. All five automatically allocated points then go into a branch they had ignored. The protagonist doubts that this is random and explicitly says they would have chosen another branch.

**Limit of the inference:** the text does not provide the generator's code, so it does not establish manipulated randomness or an actual rule change. Nor does it demonstrate an independent verifier consistently applying a contract known in advance. Part of the explanation is the protagonist's interpretation. The description discovered later could clarify an old rule or reflect an ad hoc decision; the excerpt does not resolve this.

**Backlog delta TR2-03 — immutable reward contract and checks for choice steering.** Novelty, completion, and resource reward have separate records. A reward refers to the rule version, scope of the verified result, and modifiers applied. If progression is personalized, the player is told that this mode is active; a hidden change to development is not presented as random luck. Evaluator evolution does not redefine achievements already earned.

**Acceptance check:** the same evidence under the same contract and relevant prerequisites produces the same assessment; independent seeds allow the claimed randomness to be tested. A new description does not retroactively turn success into failure. “Agent performance improved” does not increase solely because the task wording changed.

For the concept, TR-S14 remains an excellent metaphor for the difference between a map and exploration. Cite it together with this limitation, rather than as fictional confirmation of our architecture.

<a id="4-зрозуміла-взаємодія-ще-не-пояснює-народження-нового-правила"></a>
## 4. An understandable interaction does not yet explain the birth of a new rule

**New source:** V5 §8, P169–196. The protagonist chooses an “absorbing” arena: it should restrain exit while allowing territorial effects through. This explains why influence can enter through an open portal; the environment begins to change. But after a third party attempts a breakthrough, the System announces a 981-year effect, an anomaly, its relocation, and the creation of a new structure. The block read contains no rule from which that exact duration, type, or stabilization procedure can be derived.

**What we correct:** TR-S12 strongly supports “an overlooked boundary became a channel of influence.” It provides weaker support for the next claim, “new geography is a computed and verified consequence.” At this point a separate System intervention begins, explained only through its messages.

**Backlog delta TR2-04 — separate consequence from ontology change.** A local cascade follows existing capabilities. Creating a new kind of place, institution, or agent is a separate candidate with semantics, authority to introduce it, limits, migration, and verification. The narrator must not disguise such a transition as an ordinary object effect.

**Acceptance check:** every node E01–E08 of the first slice is explained by current rules. A petition to give a public place its own stewardship does not establish that stewardship. It needs a separate accepted epoch transition; rejecting the candidate leaves everything the player already did with the bench true.

<a id="5-у-випадкової-геніальності-приховані-ресурси-привілеї-й-чужа-праця"></a>
## 5. “Accidental genius” hides resources, privileges, and other people's work

**New source:** V5 §8, P151–179; V6 §4, P167–194 and P301–304. The portal scene relies on a rare artifact with a daily recharge, personal abilities, an equipped Abode, an expensive arena form, and a combat group. Some participants object and are then forced to stop arguing. The cosmic survey follows substantial preparation: information from others, a collective ritual, a billion units of power, and a failed attempt with resource loss and bodily backlash. A later barrier still requires help from a powerful acquaintance.

**Rejected hypothesis:** these scenes do not establish cost-free growth from a small gesture by an ordinary character. They show a side effect of substantial accumulated capacity. In V5 §8, P140, the protagonist separately acknowledges claiming control over an earlier spontaneous result they did not actually control.

**Backlog delta TR2-05 — account for prerequisites and discovery attribution.** For every demonstration of a remarkable outcome, record the full set of required permissions, objects, prior history, time, help, and costs. Separate the originator of the idea, the executors, others' resources, and external chance. The first cascade available to a newcomer must actually work with starting capabilities, rather than an invisible administrative demo bonus.

**Acceptance check:** removing a required resource or consent from the control run removes the corresponding effect. A newcomer with genuinely available conditions can reproduce a simple combination. “The scenario author likes this character” is not a prerequisite. An NPC's or future human partner's right to refuse persists even if that prevents the showcase story from completing.

In the town scenario, an umbrella can initiate local change, but Yaryna, Ilka, the owner, and the schedule do not become decorative extras in the player's success.

<a id="6-світ-із-думок--сильна-фантазія-небезпечний-контракт-оптимізатора"></a>
## 6. A world made from thoughts is a powerful fantasy and a dangerous optimizer contract

**New source, complete section:** V4 §1. P42–44 acknowledge earlier deception of the protagonist and hidden direction of their attention. P87–96 explain the world's creation by people and explicitly place racial diversity in conflict with people's experiences. P121–129 describe deliberate use of an external threat and targeted interventions to draw people into capsules. P133–139 assert that imprecise human conceptions shape phenomena's properties, while even the interlocutor does not know who ultimately controls the world.

**Limit of the inference:** this is an explanation by a character with evident goals, not a neutral technical protocol. The protagonist expresses doubt within the chapter; statements about reality, inevitable death, and care receive no independent demonstration. The narrative ability to perceive auras is likewise not equivalent to scientific verification of truthfulness.

**Backlog delta TR2-06 — separate domains of knowledge, imagination, and authority.** A thought, assumption, utterance, rule proposal, and authorized action are stored as different entities. In ordinary play, an unguarded thought does not become world law. A mechanic in which imagination creates properties is possible as a separate voluntary mode with an explicit scope. System improvement must not secretly alter rewards, access, or emotional pressure to force a preferred diversity of behavior.

**Acceptance check:** identical authorized actions in identical states do not punish someone for expressing doubt or declining to experiment. An NPC can be mistaken or manipulative as a character, but its assertion does not enter the authoritative log as fact without appropriate evidence. An RSI candidate with greater diversity but hidden coercion or violated participation terms is rejected regardless of its average engagement score.

An additional scale correction: in P76, the interlocutor explicitly distinguishes small accidents from global branches of the world. This supports our allowance for no cascade, although the explanation remains that character's theory. “Can change the world” does not mean “must change it after every unusual action.”

## Recommended document changes after this pass

| Decision | What to revise | Research priority |
|---|---|---|
| TR2-01 | Core evolution acceptance criteria: novelty or profit does not offset an independent failure | Before the first promotion cycle |
| TR2-02 | Task and temporary-role contracts: proposed / accepted / fulfilled are separate | In the first single-player slice |
| TR2-03 | Reward issuance: fixed criterion, separate novelty, personalization audit | Before the first persistent progression |
| TR2-04 | Boundary between runtime consequence and new ontology; a petition is not a migration | Before the first epoch transition |
| TR2-05 | Complete accounting of prerequisites, help, and attribution; control run without privileges | In the first single-player slice |
| TR2-06 | Knowledge and authority types; non-negotiable participation terms in RSI evaluation | Before adapting rules to the player |

This pass does not require abandoning a living world. It clarifies what must be demonstrated: a powerful story does not replace another person's ability to make their own choice; a good explanation does not replace a causal trace; the System's creativity does not replace verified Core improvement.
